# Reproduced result summary

Generated by `python reproduce.py` on the final package.

## Exact finite families
All reported `d=4` compatibility classes are obtained by exhaustive enumeration of the 65,535 nonempty global relations. The parity family has task opacity 1 at every proper order and rank 4. Sparse XOR and the hypergraph demonstration collapse at rank 3, while parity, one-hot, and the threshold example require rank 4.

## Random decision benchmark
There are 250 targets at each observation order (750 paired checks total). Every implemented radius/opacity regret-bound audit passed. HCAT is not an average-regret dominator: at k=1 its mean realized regret is higher than the join baseline. Its designed advantage is certified worst-case utility. Mean HCAT-minus-join guaranteed-utility gains are:
- k=1: 0.0780 (one-sided paired Wilcoxon p=0.00282386).
- k=2: 1.3232 (one-sided paired Wilcoxon p=2.78099e-07).
- k=3: 0.6904 (one-sided paired Wilcoxon p=0.0128391).

## Real-data structural audit
The WDBC transformation uses three median-binarized selected features plus the binary target, yielding a four-coordinate support. It is a structural audit, not a classifier or clinical decision system. Exact rank is 4; all 200 row-bootstrap resamples also yield rank 4.

## Interpretation rule
Do not state that HCAT universally outperforms relational, marginal, PID, bisimulation, or robust-optimization methods. The supported claim is that HCAT explicitly quantifies the compatible global class under bounded-order observation and can convert its operational radius into a decision certificate.
